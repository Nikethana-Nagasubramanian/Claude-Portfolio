Scout — Open Source

From 230 Roles Across 5 Sources to 3 Worth My Time

I built Scout after realizing that job searching was not a discovery problem. It was a continuity problem. The role was in one place, the resume in another, and the reasoning behind each application disappeared between them.

RoleSolo Product Designer & Builder

StatusFree & Open Source

ScopeProduct Strategy, UX, Visual Design, React/TypeScript Implementation

Problem Statement

Job searching is scattered across job boards, company sites, inbox alerts, resume files, and application trackers. Finding the role is only the beginning; the work required to decide, tailor, and apply is fragmented too.

The Product Thesis

Scout is an open-source workspace that keeps the job, the fit assessment, the resume edits, and the application connected from discovery through submission.

01The Bet

Go Beyond the Traditional LinkedIn Search

Tell Scout where to look, then let it bring the worthwhile opportunities together.

Scout can look through Gmail job alerts, public discovery feeds, curated lists, company career pages, and any other resource I point it toward. It extracts the roles, removes duplicates, checks basic fit and eligibility, and separates the opportunities worth my time from the noise.

From there, the job becomes the centre of the workflow. Scout keeps the role, profile match, resume edits, and cover letter connected instead of making me restart the context in a different tool at every step.

02Design-Engineering Decisions

Make uncertainty visible, then keep context intact

The product is shaped around the moments where a job-search copilot could quietly make the wrong call.

Source-directed discovery. Users choose where Scout looks — from Gmail alerts and public feeds to curated pages and company boards — so the search has a clear boundary.

Eligibility is not a guess. Duplicate roles are consolidated, while fit and eligibility checks are surfaced as signals that may need verification rather than presented as fact.

Approval stays human. Resume suggestions show the original line, the proposed revision, and the evidence behind it. Scout can help prepare the application, but it does not silently rewrite or submit one.

One role, one thread of context. The selected role carries through profile matching, resume edits, and cover-letter generation so each step builds on the last.

What Scout does not automate

Scout is deliberately not an auto-apply tool. Eligibility checks can be incomplete, job data can become stale, and AI suggestions still need human approval. The product is designed to make those uncertainties visible rather than hide them.

05Color

Color explains what changed—and what to trust

06The System at Work

From application software to an editorial workspace

The early exploration established the editorial language. The final direction gave the workspace a stronger olive frame.

  1. A stronger frame

    The olive sidebar adds contrast and separates navigation from the document.

  2. An editorial hierarchy

    Serif titles and monospace labels carry through from exploration to final.

  3. Reviewable suggestions

    Layered notes keep the original, revision, and evidence together in both directions.
